Warm up and lift with control

  • Take 5–10 minutes of easy walking or cycling, including on leg day if comfortable. Before the first big lift, do one or two lighter practice sets. Your chart calls for a treadmill run on non-leg days. Keep that easy if you choose to run.
  • Use a controlled lowering phase and a comfortable range. Exhale through the effort and inhale on the return. End the set when your technique changes or you can no longer control the load.
  • Rest about 2 minutes for presses, squats, rows and hinges. Allow more if your next set needs it. Rest roughly 60–90 seconds for smaller lifts and core work.
  • Stop an exercise if you feel sharp or worsening pain. Have your trainer check unfamiliar movements, especially the squat and hip hinge. Get medical advice for persistent pain or an active injury before increasing load.

Recover between sessions

  • Day 7 is recovery. Take an easy walk and do gentle mobility if you feel like it. Aim for at least 7 hours of sleep if you’re an adult, drink water regularly, and include a protein source in meals.
  • If soreness changes your movement or your performance keeps dropping, take an extra recovery day or reduce working sets. Skip missed sessions or resume with the next one you can do. Avoid stacking two sessions to catch up.
  • Keep day 6 pressing lighter than day 4. Day 5 uses a small amount of back work after hamstrings. Keep it easy when you’re still recovering from day 3.
  • Increase weight by the smallest available step when you reach the top of the rep range on every set and can still do 2 clean reps. If the next jump is too large, stay at the current weight and repeat the target.
